# Locked matched-nonce and phase-rotation experiment

This addendum was written after the seven-cipher article and its earlier data
were inspected, and before generating master-key indices 440--519. The two
core-round findings motivate a targeted mechanistic test. Its two primary
endpoints are prospective only for this new cohort. The previous six-cipher
and Salsa20 transfer protocols and data are unchanged.

## Cipher interfaces and common sampling

- Ciphers: IETF ChaCha20 and original Salsa20, each with 256-bit keys and
  core-round settings 2, 4, and 20. Twenty rounds is standard; the others
  are research variants. Generate the first 4096 keystream bits at counters
  0--7, yielding eight 512-bit cipher blocks.
- Use 80 new master-key indices 440--519, master seed 20260721. Derive the
  ChaCha20 and Salsa20 keys and their oracle secrets with separate BLAKE2b
  domains from each master key. Derive one *common* set of four 64-bit base
  nonces per master-key index from a cipher-independent SHAKE256 domain.
- Use the four single-bit and four adjacent four-bit masks returned by
  `standard_iv_validation._mask_sets(8)`, giving 16 nonce pairs in each
  family. The same nonce values and masks are used for both ciphers and
  all round settings. Check that the joint eight-mask nonce-pair graph is
  four disjoint eight-edge stars.
- Salsa20 receives each 64-bit nonce directly. ChaCha20 receives a 96-bit
  IETF nonce with its first 32 bits fixed at zero and the common 64-bit
  nonce in the remaining positions. With counters below 2^32, this is
  byte-for-byte the original ChaCha 64-bit-nonce/64-bit-counter state layout.
  Before this protocol was written, the 20-round mapping was checked
  against `cryptography`'s independent original ChaCha20 implementation for
  three keys/nonces and 512 output bytes per case. Salsa20/20 had already
  passed the independent libsodium vector gate.
- The control assigns a deterministic SHAKE256 stream to each unique nonce,
  cipher, key, and round setting. A repeated base nonce reuses the same
  stream across masks. For each pair, take XOR HWs of 32 successive,
  disjoint 128-bit output blocks. Power is averaged across all 16 pairs
  before spectral normalization. One master-key index is the replication
  unit; cipher keys are domain-separated but its base nonces are shared.

## Phase intervention

For each key, cipher, round setting, and mask family, take the 16 by 32
matrix of HWs and reshape it to 16 pairs by eight 512-bit blocks by four
128-bit positions. Make 128 independent intervention replicates. In each
replicate, independently choose one of four cyclic rotations for every
pair and every 512-bit block. Apply the **same** rotation schedule to the
observed and matched-control HW matrices. This breaks alignment of the
four positions across blocks while preserving the four-value order up to
rotation within every block. It preserves each trajectory's multiset,
mean, variance, total Fourier power, and DC share exactly; trajectory
length stays T=32. Store every rotation schedule in the raw arrays.

Let `Delta_original = H_noDC(observed) - H_noDC(control)` for a key and
cell. Let `Delta_rotated` be the mean of the 128 observed-minus-control
H_noDC differences after paired rotations. Define the signed alignment
effect `L = Delta_original - Delta_rotated`. A negative L means coherent
position alignment lowers the observed-minus-control AC entropy. Also
compute full-spectrum entropy, DC share, and the four-position HW range
before and after rotation. Report original, rotated, and L values.

## Fixed inference and secondary checks

1. The two primary endpoints are L at **two rounds** for ChaCha20 and L
   at **two rounds** for Salsa20, using only single-bit masks and all 32
   HW blocks. Each gets a two-sided whole-key sign-flip test with 200,000
   random sign vectors and a plus-one Monte Carlo p-value. Correct the two
   tests together by Holm. Their hypotheses were chosen after the old
   cipher results, but before seeing any data from indices 440--519.
2. Use 5000 paired whole-master-key-index bootstrap resamples for
   pointwise 95% intervals of each cell's means and of Salsa20-minus-
   ChaCha20 differences under the now-matched nonce domain. The direct
   cross-core comparisons are descriptive, not additional confirmatory
   tests.
3. Four- and 20-round settings, adjacent four-bit masks, raw and rotated
   phase ranges, full-spectrum entropy, and residual AC contrasts after
   rotation are descriptive specificity and sensitivity checks. A
   confidence interval covering zero is not an equivalence claim.

The transformation identifies the contribution of phase alignment to this
*statistic*. It does not alter the cipher and cannot by itself quantify
cryptanalytic attack advantage. A residual post-rotation AC contrast would
show that phase alignment is not the only structure measured by H_noDC.
