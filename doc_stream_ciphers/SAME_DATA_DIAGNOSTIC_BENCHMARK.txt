# Fixed same-data diagnostic benchmark plan

This plan was written after the results of the matched-nonce phase study were
known, but before calculating the comparator statistics below. It is a
**descriptive secondary analysis**, not a prospective hypothesis test. No
new keys or IV pairs are introduced. All statistics use exactly the
ChaCha20 and Salsa20 observed/control streams from master-key indices
440--519, with the single-bit 64-bit-nonce masks in
`MATCHED_NONCE_PHASE_PROTOCOL.md`, the first 4096 output bits, and the
same 16 nonce pairs per key and configuration. Compare 2, 4, and 20 core
rounds. Twenty is standard; 2 and 4 are research variants.

## Comparators and common unit

For each key, core, and round setting, regenerate the deterministic output
pairs and graph-matched SHAKE256 control specified in the locked phase
protocol. Verify all 16 by 32 disjoint 128-bit XOR-HW values against the
archived raw arrays **before** computing any benchmark result. Every
statistic is computed once per key from the same 16 paired trajectories;
the key, not the pair or block, is the replication unit.

1. `mean_hw`: mean XOR HW over all 16 by 32 blocks. The observed-minus-
   control difference measures average avalanche deficit or excess in bits
   per 128 output bits. It is unchanged by any permutation of blocks.
2. `sac_rms`: for each of the 512 output-bit positions within a cipher
   block, estimate the flip probability over 16 pairs by eight blocks
   (128 trials). Take the root mean square deviation of those 512
   probabilities from 1/2. This is a finite-sample strict-avalanche-style
   deviation, **not** a formal SAC proof. Compare observed and control
   scores per key. Store the 512 bit-flip counts for both streams, each
   count between 0 and 128. Bit positions follow byte order with the most
   significant bit first.
3. `lag4`: center each 32-HW trajectory by its own mean, sum lag-four
   cross-products over pairs and valid positions, and divide by total
   centered energy over all pairs and all 32 positions. Compare observed
   and control values per key. This targets the four-position periodicity.
4. `phase_range`: range of the four position means in each 512-bit output
   block, pooled over 16 pairs and eight blocks; compare observed and
   control per key. This directly exposes position alignment.
5. `hmp`, `p0`, and normalized `hnodc`: same power-averaged spectral
   definitions as in the article, recalculated from the archived HW arrays.
   These distinguish a full-spectrum response from DC share and AC
   allocation. Compare observed and control per key.

For each observed-minus-control metric, report the 80-key mean and
pointwise 95% percentile intervals from 5000 whole-key bootstrap
resamples. Reuse the same bootstrap indices across all metrics within a
cipher and round setting. No significance tests, familywise correction,
or ranking by p-value are claimed for this retrospective comparison.
The 2-round rows illustrate detection and localization; 4- and 20-round
rows check whether effects persist at greater mixing. Because metrics
have different units, compare their behavior and information content, not
their raw numerical magnitudes. In particular, do not claim that spectral
entropy is more sensitive unless the results directly support that claim.

The benchmark's purpose is to test whether the phase-aware spectral view
adds an interpretable DC/AC distinction beyond simpler HW, bitwise
avalanche, autocorrelation, and phase-range summaries on identical data.
