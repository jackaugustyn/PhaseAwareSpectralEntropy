# Fixed empirical prediction models for the two-round ARX spectrum

Written on 2026-10-31 after the earlier observational and intervention
studies, before fitting these prediction models or generating their new
evaluation cohort. This is a locally fixed prospective follow-up, not a
public preregistration. Freeze this text and generator/analysis sources
before the final run. Do not adapt a model after seeing evaluation data.

## Question and scope

Test whether empirical 32-bit position profiles plus independently sampled
residuals predict the finite-sample spectrum, 512-bit fundamental fraction,
and nonce-word entropy gap of standard additive two-round ChaCha20 and
Salsa20. The two fixed models differ only in whether training-key and
base-nonce profiles are retained. Neither is a purely analytic cipher model.
No detector-power, full-round-security, or new-attack claim is sought.

## Training data and fixed models

- Training input is only the observed, two-round slice of
  data/arx_resolution_validation/arx_resolution_validation_raw.npz:
  80 key pairs, indices 840--919, both ciphers, all 64 singleton nonce masks,
  four base nonces, 16 consecutive 512-bit blocks and 16 HW32 words per block.
  Confirm the existing raw hash and key indices before fitting. Do not use
  this cohort's four-round slice or any new evaluation observations.
- M0: for each cipher, global nonce mask and output word, fit its empirical
  HW32 distribution on all 80*4*16=5120 training observations. Draw each
  new key/mask/base/block/word value independently from that marginal.
- M1: for each simulated key pair draw one training-key index uniformly,
  shared by both ciphers, every mask and all four base slots. Within that
  selected key, retain the four original base-nonce slots. For each cipher,
  mask, base and output word, fit the empirical distribution over its 16
  blocks; draw every new block/word value independently from that layer.
  This is equivalently conditional mean plus an empirical centered residual.
- Use empirical distributions without smoothing, parametric fitting,
  tuning or holdout corrections. Population moments of each empirical
  distribution use its empirical denominator (5120 for M0, 16 for M1).
  Both models preserve training-support zeros. The known Salsa20 zero-word
  and high-bit feed-forward identities provide structural context, not a
  derivation of the entire fitted marginal distribution.
- M0 removes key/base random effects as well as residual word/block
  dependence. M1 retains empirical between-key and between-base profiles,
  including their shared pattern across masks, but removes residual
  dependence between words and blocks. M1's conditional distributions have
  only 16 training blocks, some repeated because of the operation graph;
  their fitted variation is not a population estimate based on independent
  blocks. Equal weighting of complete keys is retained in both fits.

## Expected spectrum and finite Monte Carlo prediction

For segment width s in {32,64,128}, let L=512/s, B=16, T=B*L. The segment
mean is the sum of its constituent HW32 means and its model residual
variance is the sum of their variances. Repeat the L-position mean profile
over B output blocks. Under independent residuals, for every DFT bin k:
  E[P_k | layer] = |DFT(mu_repeat)_k|^2 + sum_t variance_t.
The Q=4 power average has this same expectation. For M0 there is one
profile per mask. For M1 compute this expression separately for every
training key and base, then average over those layers. Do not replace M1's
mean squared Fourier magnitude by the Fourier magnitude of a pooled mean.
Archive the expected power per mask and normalized expected-power summaries.
Entropy of this normalized expectation is not expected finite-sample entropy.

- Simulate exactly 4096 complete pseudo-key pairs per model, in batches of
  32. Each pseudo-key contains 64 masks, Q=4 base trajectories, 16 blocks,
  and 16 HW32 words. Generate one HW32 sample and aggregate it to all three
  resolutions, preserving the comparison across widths.
- Sampling RNG: NumPy default_rng(SeedSequence([2026103101, model_id,
  family_id])), where model_id is 0/1 and family_id is 0 for ChaCha20 and
  1 for Salsa20. M0 samples an index in the pooled 5120-value marginal;
  M1 samples one of the 16 training-block values within its selected layer.
  Draw the 4096 M1 training-key indices once with seed 2026103102 and retain
  that array. Sampling is independent across new word/block residuals.
- The finite-model reference consists of independent Binomial(32,1/2)
  HW32 values, drawn with SeedSequence([2026103103,family_id]). Reuse the
  same reference sequence for both models, and aggregate it to all widths.
  This reference targets mean metrics for ideal independent differences;
  the simulation does not emulate joint nonce-vertex or key covariance.
- For each pseudo-key and mask, pool its four complete two-sided Fourier
  powers before normalization. Apply the exact existing H_MP, normalized
  AC entropy and fundamental/harmonic definitions. Average masks within
  the pseudo-key. Compute G by subtracting model-reference H_MP for first
  nonce-word masks 0--31 from second-word masks 32--63, pairing offsets.
  F512 is (P[B]+P[T-B])/sum(P[1:]) for each mask before averaging; DeltaF512
  subtracts the corresponding finite reference.
- Archive pseudo-key scalar metrics, finite mean normalized full/AC
  spectra and Monte Carlo standard errors, expected-power predictions,
  fit parameters, seeds, and hashes before generating evaluation streams.
  No comparison with heldout data may change the fit or MC budget.

## Independent evaluation cohort

- Use master indices 1200--1279 and seed 20261031 with the existing
  publication_study._key_material helper. Independent per-family keys and
  reference secrets are BLAKE2b of respectively
  spectral-prediction-key-v1:<family>: || master and
  spectral-prediction-secret-v1:<family>: || master (32 output bytes).
- Four common 64-bit base nonces come from SHAKE256 of
  spectral-prediction-nonces-v1: || master (32 output bytes). Flip all 64
  single bits using global MSB-first byte numbering. Check that the four
  65-vertex stars are disjoint. ChaCha20 uses 0^32 || nonce, Salsa20 the
  shared nonce. Use standard additive output at exactly two core rounds.
- Emit 1024 bytes per nonce, counters 0--15. For each nonce vertex, the
  graph-matched reference emits SHAKE256 of
  spectral-prediction-oracle-v1:<family>: || secret || uint16_be(2) || nonce,
  taking 1024 bytes. Reuse streams at repeated vertices. Record observed
  and reference HW32 with axes key,cipher,mask,base,block,word.
- Smoke indices 1280--1281 may be used only under /tmp, with 32 MC
  pseudo-keys per model. Smoke outputs cannot be used for final model fits,
  MC summaries or evaluation. Do not select methods or endpoints using them.

## Fixed reporting and uncertainty

Report all six cipher-by-resolution cells for both M0 and M1. The principal
scalar summaries are predicted and observed G, DeltaF512, their signed
errors (observed minus prediction), and the signs of G. Report H_MP, DC
share, normalized AC entropy and harmonic-share summaries descriptively.
The ChaCha20 128-bit gap has no required sign. Do not choose a successful
model or resolution after inspecting the evaluation cohort.

For spectra, normalize each key/mask power vector before averaging masks
and keys. Report full normalized spectra and separately normalized AC
spectra for model, finite reference and evaluation data. Report the L1
distance between predicted and observed mean AC spectra in every cell.
Keep analytic normalized-expected-power profiles separate from finite MC
profiles; neither may silently replace the other.

Use 5000 whole-evaluation-key bootstrap resamples, seed 2026103104,
for pointwise 95% intervals of observed means, signed errors and spectral
L1 errors. Draw one shared key-index matrix across ciphers, widths and
models. These intervals are conditional on the fixed 80-key training fit
and fixed MC estimate; they do not include training uncertainty. Report
MC standard errors separately, from the 4096 pseudo-key values (or the
equivalent first and second moments for per-bin profiles).

No p values, post-hoc success margins, equivalence claims or calibrated
detector comparisons are planned. Compare both models with all evaluation
results and describe failures. A difference between M0 and M1 measures the
effect of retaining empirical conditional profiles within this model
family; it does not isolate all real cipher dependence or establish that
the residuals are independent.

## Integrity and reproducibility

Write only new files under data/arx_spectral_prediction and the new named
study/test/manuscript files. Freeze training input, protocol and analysis
source hashes. Final evaluation generation requires a complete frozen
prediction manifest. Per-key checkpoints may resume only with an identical
fingerprint; never mix cohort, source or model versions. Verify analytic
expected power against exact small-support enumeration, marginal sampling,
aggregation, Q-before-normalization, finite entropy conventions, and
the separate training/evaluation paths before final generation. Independently
audit the fitted parameters, model calculations, evaluation streams and
reported statistics. Retain any model discrepancy in the final report.
