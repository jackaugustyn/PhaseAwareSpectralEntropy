# Locked Salsa20 transfer protocol for the seven-cipher article

This addendum is fixed after inspection of the six-cipher results and before
generating any Salsa20 research trajectories. The original six-cipher protocol
and its test families remain unchanged. Salsa20 is a prospective *transfer*
test of the ChaCha20 block-position interpretation, not an independent blind
discovery of that interpretation.

## Cipher and validation gate

- Original Salsa20 with a 256-bit key, 64-bit nonce, 64-bit block counter from
  zero, and even core-round grid `2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 20`. Only 20 rounds is the
  standard full configuration. All shorter cores are research variants.
- The standard implementation must match the archived libsodium 1.0.18
  `crypto_stream_salsa20` vectors for zero and nonzero keys/nonces, including
  a vector crossing a 512-bit block boundary. An independent literal
  columnround/rowround translation must agree for every reduced round count.
  These checks were completed before this protocol was written. Results are
  not generated if either gate fails.

## Sampling and control

- Use 80 new master-key indices `360..439` with the existing master seed
  `20260721`. These indices do not overlap the six-cipher cohort `280..359`.
  Family-separated BLAKE2b and SHAKE256 calls in the six-cipher utilities
  derive the key, oracle secret, and four base nonces. The Salsa20 family name
  makes all derived material distinct from prior families.
- Use four single-bit nonce masks and four adjacent four-bit masks at the
  positions returned by `standard_iv_validation._mask_sets(8)`. Each family
  gives 16 paired nonces per key; reuse the same pairs at all round counts.
  Confirm that the joint pair graph has four disjoint eight-edge stars.
- Generate 4096 output bits per nonce and round count. For every pair, take
  XOR Hamming weights in 32 disjoint consecutive 128-bit blocks. Analyze the
  full 32-block sequence and, descriptively, the last 16 blocks.
- The control maps each unique nonce, key, family, and round count to one
  SHAKE256 stream and reuses that stream across the pair graph. Compute all
  spectra from power averaged over the 16 trajectories before normalization,
  exactly as in the six-cipher protocol. The key is the sampling unit.
- Derive pointwise 95% intervals by 5000 whole-key bootstrap resamples.
  Use the same H_MP, DC share, normalized H_noDC, Q8, and symmetric exact
  DC/AC attribution definitions as in the six-cipher experiment.

## Planned transfer endpoints

One separate three-test Salsa20 family uses only single-bit masks and the
full 32-block window:

1. At two rounds, observed-minus-control normalized H_noDC.
2. At two rounds, observed-minus-control four-position mean-HW range within
   a 512-bit output block. For each key, first average each position over
   all 16 nonce pairs and eight 512-bit blocks, then subtract minimum from
   maximum; compare that range with its matched-control range.
3. At the standard 20 rounds, observed-minus-control full-spectrum H_MP.

All three receive two-sided whole-key sign-flip tests with 200,000 random
sign vectors each, plus-one Monte Carlo p-values, and Holm correction within
this Salsa20 family. This family was chosen after the ChaCha20 result was
known; its corrected p-values apply to fresh Salsa20 data only. A null
standard-setting test cannot establish equivalence.

Other round counts, the four-bit-mask family, final-16-block windows,
first-block Hamming weight, DC/AC attribution, pooled Q8, and aggregation of
four 128-bit HW values into each of eight 512-bit HW values are descriptive.
The 512-bit aggregation changes trajectory length from 32 to 8, so its
entropy contrast is a representation sensitivity check, not the same
estimand. Comparisons of Salsa20 and ChaCha20 effect sizes are descriptive;
their master-key cohorts differ. The extended article will preserve the
original six planned standard tests and three fresh-key replications as
separate prior families, rather than relabel them as a prospective seven-cipher
family.
