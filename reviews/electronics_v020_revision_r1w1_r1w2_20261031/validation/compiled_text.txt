Experimental Diagnostics of IV Diffusion

Abstract
1
Early nonce responses reflect the round function, output finalization, and measurement.
2
We separate these stages in reduced ChaCha20 and Salsa20 using exact algebra and paired
3
experiments on independent key cohorts. Both two-round cores exhibit recurring 512-bit
4
structure; within-block rotations remove approximately 96% and 94% of their nonzero-
5
frequency entropy contrasts. An independent scan confirms a Salsa20/2 nonce-word
6
entropy gap of 0.79059 bits. This asymmetry precedes feed-forward, while modular addi-
7
tion increases it and introduces a predicted mixed nonce interaction. A conditional identity
8
bounds the mean singleton response of Salsa20/2 output word 7 to flips in the second nonce
9
word by 31/32 changed bits, with the remaining state fixed, and gives its exact local rank.
10
A third-round update ends the sampled conditional affinity of the unfinalized word-7 map
11
while substantial block-scale structure persists in standard output. Two frozen empirical
12
models predict entropy-gap signs on new keys; retaining conditional positional profiles
13
improves mean nonzero-frequency spectra at all tested resolutions, but not every entropy
14
gap. Spectral entropy thus interprets construction-linked responses without establishing
15
superior detector sensitivity, an attack, or full-round insecurity.
16
Keywords: experimental cryptanalysis; stream ciphers; IV diffusion; ChaCha20; Salsa20;
17
avalanche effect; spectral diagnostics; feed-forward
18
1. Introduction
19
Nonce diffusion measurements combine the round function, feed-forward, and output
20
aggregation. ChaCha20 and Salsa20 share addition, rotation, and exclusive OR (XOR),
21
termed ARX operations, but differ in state layout and round schedule [1,2]. Reduced
22
rounds allow these stages to be examined separately.
23
We hold the key fixed, perturb the public initialization vector (IV), here the nonce, and
24
measure ordered Hamming weights (HW) of output differences [3,4]. Conditional identities,
25
paired finalizations, and predictive controls connect local mechanisms to measured spectra.
26
Spectral entropy summarizes these responses rather than keystream entropy or security;
27
direct diagnostics provide a benchmark.
28

3. Materials and Methods
53
3.1. Common Inputs, References, and Independent Cohorts
54
Each principal cohort has 80 matched pairs of separately derived 256-bit cipher keys
55
and four shared 64-bit base nonces v per pair. Salsa20 uses v; ChaCha20 uses 032∥v; counters
56
start at zero. BLAKE2b domains separate keys and control secrets [32]; SHAKE256 derives
57
nonces and reference streams [33]. Each key/cipher/configuration reuses one reference
58
stream per nonce, with collision-checked disjoint base/variant groups. Under an ideal
59
random-function model, distinct variants give independent uniform differences despite
60
sharing a base. SHAKE256 realizes the reuse pattern computationally, without proving
61
ideal randomness.
62
Table 1 separates the cohorts. Alignment uses singleton masks at bytewise most-
63
significant-bit (MSB) first positions 0, 21, 42, 63; other cohorts scan all 64 singletons with
64
paired nonce-word offsets. State-word bits are least-significant-bit (LSB) first. Protocols
65
preceded generation; independent cohorts mean new keys, not another team. Standard
66
outputs passed vectors or independent library checks; reduced and odd-round cores were
67
checked against independent scalar rounds.

4. Results
157
4.1. Round Function: Recurring Structure and Conditional Rank
158
Both two-round cores show recurring 512-bit structure (Figure 1a). Their four-position
159
range exceeds the reference by 17.87 bits [17.53, 18.21] for ChaCha20 and 30.41 [30.03,
160
30.78] for Salsa20. Their mean HW deficits are 21.77 and 37.57 bits per 128-bit segment,
161
respectively. The independent 64-mask scan confirms a Salsa20 nonce-word entropy gap
162
of +0.79059 bits (Table 2); every key-level and every offset-averaged gap is positive. The
163
descriptive ChaCha20 gap is −0.00036 bits [−0.00393, 0.00307].
164
Tracing the round schedules [1,2] distinguishes missing dependencies from weak
165
responses: absent paths prove independence, whereas present paths specify neither HW
166
nor differential probabilities. After two Salsa rounds, changing x6 cannot affect output
167
word 1; changing x7 cannot affect words 0, 1, 2, 3, 6. Modular feed-forward preserves these
168
zeros. The asymmetry follows from quarter-round update order b, c, d, a: x6 enters the

214

5. Scope and Discussion
260
The direct benchmark detects the same large two-round departures: mean HW,
261
avalanche RMS, four-position range, lag-four correlation, and both entropy summaries
262
have pointwise intervals excluding zero. Their four- and 20-round intervals include zero.
263
Thus spectral decomposition explains aspects of an already detectable response; no cali-
264
brated detector-power advantage was established. Small higher-round contrasts, including
265
occasional nonzero pointwise effects in other cohorts, establish neither simultaneous equiv-
266
alence nor security.
267
Ancillary studies delimit the diagnostic. The initial seven-cipher comparison (Grain-
268
128, Trivium, MICKEY 2.0, HC-128, Rabbit, ChaCha20, Salsa20; 80 keys each) used four
269
bases, four distributed singleton masks and 4096-bit windows (Q = 16, s = 128). All
270
standard-setting mean HMP contrasts were below 0.001 bits in magnitude with pointwise
271
intervals covering zero. Grain-128/0 and Trivium/64 instead showed transient contrasts
272
nearly absent from the last 16 segments; initialization counts are not comparable to ARX
273
rounds [5,37,38]. On 80 fresh Grain keys and four bases, the zero-clock bit-64-minus-bit-
274
0 gap replicated at +0.09526 bits [0.08921, 0.10126], p = 10−5, and became small after
275
standard initialization.
276
An 80-key Trivium pilot used degree-d derivatives (d = 2, 4, 6) over 512 bits: the first d
277
LSB-first positions of {0, 3, . . . , 69}, with bases zero outside the cube or setting one of bits
278
72, 75, 78. Deficits survived 12-cell max-|t| correction at 192/384 clocks but not 768/1152;
279
the published attack’s key conditions were not imposed [39].
280
A5/1 used 80 keys, four frames, 22 singleton masks and 114 output bits. The 20-clock
281
flip-fraction test was unresolved (p = 0.719). At 100 standard clocks, none of 44 mask-
282
specific flip-fraction/entropy tests (19 six-bit HW samples) survived correction (minimum
283
p = 0.669). Another 80 keys confirmed the zero-clock, LSB-first bit-21-minus-bit-0 gradient:
284
−0.04350 [−0.05433, −0.03281], p = 10−5. Grain/A5 validations used 100,000 sign flips
285
and 10,000 bootstrap resamples; Trivium/A5 searches used 50,000 whole-key max-|t| sign
286
flips. A quiet standard diagnostic thus coexists with A5/1 state-recovery weaknesses [40].
287
The ARX results concern the tested masks, block-aligned widths, windows up to 8192
288
bits, and selected rounds. Simulations and deterministic witnesses do not enlarge the key
289
cohorts. Nonstandard finalizations isolate construction effects, while empirical models
290
summarize selected observables. These measurements suggest positions and differences
291
for further analysis but provide no key hypothesis, attack complexity, or attack advantage.
292
6. Conclusions
293
Exact identities explain a weak Salsa20/2 output-word response and show why equal
294
means can conceal different conditional ranks. Paired finalizations locate the nonce-word
295
asymmetry before feed-forward and isolate an interaction introduced by modular addition.
296
Rotation, resolution, and frozen prediction controls connect these construction effects to
297
spectral summaries. The third-round experiment shows that conditional affinity of the
298
unfinalized word-7 map can end while block-scale structure persists in standard output.
299
These results diagnose reduced-round mechanisms without establishing superior detection
300
or full-round vulnerability.
