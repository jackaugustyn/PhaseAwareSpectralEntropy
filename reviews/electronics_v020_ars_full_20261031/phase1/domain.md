## Contract Paraphrase

D1 concerns whether the empirical procedures warrant trust in the findings. From a domain specialist's perspective, experimental choices, treatment of observations, uncertainty reporting, and the information needed to repeat the work must support a scientifically defensible interpretation; the methodology reviewer owns this assessment.

D2 concerns the scientific correctness of the subject matter. I am responsible for checking that technical language, descriptions of established knowledge, attribution of earlier contributions, and substantive conclusions accurately reflect the relevant evidence and its limits.

D3 concerns whether the reasoning holds together from premises to conclusions. Domain knowledge can help identify unsupported inferential steps or incompatible assertions, while the designated reviewers assess whether those problems weaken the central case.

D4 concerns whether readers from neighboring specialties can understand and evaluate the work. Definitions and explanations should bridge differences in background, and any asserted relevance beyond the home discipline should have a justified basis; this dimension belongs to the perspective reviewer.

D5 concerns how effectively the presentation allows readers to follow and inspect the research. A domain perspective values precise exposition, an intelligible sequence of ideas, and visual displays that communicate their scientific meaning, while the editor evaluates the full presentation and publication requirements.

D6 concerns the importance and distinctiveness of the contribution for its intended audience. Assessing that dimension requires establishing what the work adds to existing knowledge and how it relates to the publication's remit; the editor owns that judgment, and this plan makes no venue-alignment determination.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Check technical definitions, descriptions of cryptographic constructions and experimental quantities, interpretation of statistical and spectral evidence, distinctions between observed structure and security implications, and faithful attribution and characterization of relevant prior work against the available evidence.
what_triggers_block: A material but repairable factual error, misrepresentation of prior work, or unsupported domain interpretation changes the meaning or scope of a substantive conclusion and requires correction before the contribution can be assessed reliably.
what_triggers_warn: A localized terminology ambiguity, incomplete qualification, or imprecise attribution needs clarification but leaves the substantive domain interpretation intact.
what_triggers_fatal: A demonstrably false foundational domain premise invalidates the central contribution and cannot be remedied without replacing that contribution or its underlying scientific basis.
